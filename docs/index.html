Jarvis: Your Own Team of AI Experts

A personal assistant, a project manager, a doctor and a finance advisor on your computer. You talk to them by name, just like talking to a person, and they get the work done.

Source code on GitHub

Why I built Jarvis

This version of Jarvis is my first step towards a goal: giving everyone their own team of experts. It already gives you a team you can talk to, who remember you and work for you; I will keep making them more capable and more natural, until having your own team of experts feels as real as having the people themselves.

In real life, some people have a personal assistant who manages their day, a family doctor they can call any time, an advisor who looks after their money and a manager who keeps their projects on track. Most people have none of these: they are expensive, and they are not available whenever you need them.

Jarvis gives that to everyone. It is exactly like having your own personal assistant, doctor, project manager and finance advisor. Each one has a name, a voice and a personality, knows you and remembers what you told them, and you talk to them the way you would talk to a person: "Sara, I've had a headache since yesterday", "Maya, what does my day look like?". They answer out loud, ask each other for help when needed, and do the work for you.

The aim is that it feels real: not a chatbot you type into, but a team of people who are always there for you.

Everything your team can do

Every feature in this version, grouped by what it helps you with.

1Talk to them like real people

Say a name and that member answers, out loud, in their own voice. You stay with them until you name someone else.

  • Names, job titles ("doctor, …", "PA, …") and misheard names ("Sarah" → Sara) all work.
  • Or click a member's chip above the chat box; your very first message goes to Maya.
  • Before working, a member says what they will do and how long it takes ("On it, about 2 minutes"), then does it, with quiet progress under the typing dots.
  • Spoken questions get short, spoken-style answers; ask for detail and you get more.
  • Instant commands with no AI needed: "record this meeting", "stop recording", "new chat", "open Tool Hub", "mute", "unmute".

2Four ways to reach them

  • Main window: chat, history, lessons and previews.
  • Push-to-talk (Ctrl+Alt+Space) from any app: press, speak, and it stops when you finish. Speak as long as you need; a long explanation is never cut, and you can pause to think.
  • Voice card above the taskbar (Ctrl+Alt+J): hands-free answers; move it, resize it, minimise it.
  • Floating chat box (Ctrl+Shift+Space) over your work, with the world map behind it.
  • Whatever you ask on the card or in the box also shows in the main chat, in the same conversation.

3Natural voices and speech

  • 21 natural, human-sounding voices on your computer; each member has their own, which you choose on the Team page.
  • Replies start speaking while they are still being written. Adjustable speed; one switch, "Speak replies aloud", for every answer.
  • Speech recognition that copes with fast, casual speech and many accents; about 0.2 seconds per sentence on an NVIDIA graphics card.
  • Your name said right: a "say it like" spelling you can hear and adjust.
  • Music and videos pause while you talk and while the team speaks, then resume.
  • One voice at a time: members take turns and never talk over each other.
  • Your voice never leaves your computer.

4They know you and remember you

  • Each member keeps profile files about you (Sara your health profile and every lab value, Victor your budget and investment rules, Ryan your roadmap, Maya your routine and writing style) and reads them at the start of every conversation.
  • Notes about you, summarised as you talk; read, edit or erase them on the Team page.
  • Each follows the method of their field: start from your files, ask 1–3 questions if a key fact is missing, follow the right step-by-step skill, end with one concrete next step.
  • 27 built-in expert skills, plus your own skills (a short document in their Skills folder).
  • Reference files they use instead of guessing: red flags, lab ranges, screening schedules, finance formulas, verified tax rates, coding standards.

5They work at the same time, like a real team

  • Ask Maya something, then Victor straight away: both work at once, and you can keep typing.
  • Each member answers your requests in order, and tells you at once if they are still finishing an earlier one.
  • They ask each other for help, even two or three teammates at once (Ryan asks Victor about the cost and Sara about the health side together).
  • For big jobs a member starts helpers that work side by side (five lab reports, three countries' interest rates), then puts the results together.
  • They can read each other's folders but only write in their own; each keeps their own conversation.

6Maya, your personal assistant

  • Inbox triage: act now (with a draft reply), needs a teammate, read later, noise; deadlines go on the board with reminders.
  • A daily plan around your energy, a weekly review, and meeting prep briefs.
  • Emails written in your own style, never sent without your OK.
  • Personal admin: lists, renewals, important dates, travel research, packing lists, forms.
  • Morning plan and evening wrap-up, said out loud at the times you set.

7Ryan, your project manager and tech lead

  • Turns ideas into a spec and a plan before any coding, and waits for your OK.
  • Scope guard: scores new ideas against your roadmap and says "not now" when needed.
  • Project briefs, plan breakdowns with estimates, status reports and code reviews.
  • Meeting minutes with decisions and an action table; your tasks go on the board.
  • Runs the coding with Claude Code on Opus 5.5 (see Projects and coding).

8Sara, your doctor and health coach

  • Symptom checks that look for warning signs first, ask the right questions and give a clear "how urgent".
  • Lab reports read against your own history; every value kept and trends spotted.
  • Nutrition plans in your own cuisine, exercise plans, sleep and stress help, medicine information.
  • Never diagnoses with certainty or changes a prescribed dose; sends you to help for warning signs.

9Victor, your finance advisor

  • "Should I buy this?": the real cost against your income, savings and goals, with a clear verdict.
  • Budgets, debt and EMI plans, investing basics, your own investment rulebook he holds you to.
  • Tax basics from official sources, with the date checked; business analysis with best and worst cases.
  • Files your bills and has Maya remind you before they are due. Information, not regulated advice; never asks for passwords or card numbers.

10They work in your apps

  • Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Microsoft 365, Notion, Slack, Spotify and the rest of Claude's connectors: connect once on claude.ai and your team can use them.
  • The Tool Hub opens the right page and notices by itself when an app is connected.
  • You choose which member may use each app; the others don't even see it.
  • Reading is allowed, sending or changing asks you first, and deleting or paying always asks.

11Meetings and lectures

  • "Ryan, record this meeting": your microphone, the computer's sound and the screen, with a picture of every new slide or board.
  • Notes in the member's style: minutes and your tasks (Ryan), appointment notes and medicine reminders (Sara), tasks and figures (Maya, Victor).
  • During a meeting, ask "what should I answer?" and the reply appears quietly on the card, so nothing plays into the call.
  • Listen-along: during a lecture or video, ask "what did he just say?" or "explain that".
  • Videos and pictures are deleted once the notes are written, unless you choose to keep them.

12Show them anything, and they show you

  • "Sara, I want to show you something": a drop box for a PDF, photo, video or recording; she looks at it right away.
  • Videos are understood scene by scene, with a timed transcript; recordings become a transcript.
  • Or drop files on the chat or use the paperclip; they are kept in that member's folder.
  • What they make for you opens in a large preview next to the chat: documents with tables and flowcharts, pictures, PDFs, spreadsheets, web pages, video and sound. Flip through several, or open one big.

13On time, every day

  • Reminders said out loud at the right time, in the member's own voice, even with the main window closed.
  • Medicines: a reminder for every dose, for exactly the right number of days, plus the follow-up visit.
  • Bills filed with reminders before they are due; forms filled with what the team knows, leaving passwords and ID numbers to you.
  • A shared project board (To do, Doing, Done) that the team keeps up to date.

14Learn anything

  • Narrated, step-by-step lessons beside your work, each picture appearing as the voice explains it.
  • Diagrams, charts, animated graphs, curves that draw themselves, equations that transform step by step.
  • Interactive: drag points, move sliders, zoom and replay; references to your document, code or screen.
  • At your level: like I'm five, student or professional.

15Documents, code and your screen

  • They read a PDF, file or code folder you pick and quote the exact page or line.
  • "What's this error on my screen?": one picture of the window, screen or area you choose, only when you ask.
  • Files that may hold passwords or keys are never opened.

16Projects and coding

  • "Ryan, build me …": you name the new project in a popup, and its plan and every code file go into its own folder in Ryan's Projects folder.
  • Claude Code works in the background while you do something else; Ryan brings you only the real decisions, with his recommendation, in plain words ("Let Ryan install the add-on axios from the internet?").
  • Busy? Say so in your own words, or turn on Team decides: the team decides without popups and tells you everything at the end. Publishing online, anything outside the project folder and password files always wait for you.
  • A summary at the end with the test results and the changed files; give more instructions while it runs, stop it, or open it in VS Code.
  • He can start your website and leave it running for you to try.

17Web search

  • Built in for everyone, steered to trusted sources: medical authorities for Sara, official sites for Victor, official documentation for Ryan.
  • They name their sources and only give links that really came back from the search.

18Conversations and activity

  • Conversations kept by day, searchable; open an old one and continue it, each member picking up where they left off.
  • The Activity page lists everything your team did in your apps, by day, with who did it and the result.
  • Delete conversations or activity for good, any time.

19You stay in control

  • Every action is checked against your rules: Off, Ask me first or Allowed, for each app.
  • Text in emails, web pages and documents is treated as information, never as instructions.
  • Your chats, notes and files stay on your computer; your voice is never uploaded.
  • It runs on your own Claude Pro or Max plan; Jarvis never reads your Claude login.

20Easy to set up and make your own

  • A short welcome: your name and how to say it, connecting your Claude, your tools, the voices, then each member introduces themselves.
  • Rename any member and choose their voice; record your own shortcuts by pressing the keys.
  • Updating keeps your data; uninstalling removes everything Jarvis kept.

Make your team yours

Out of the box they are good in their field. What you give them makes them your assistant, your doctor, your advisor and your project manager. Each member has a folder on your computer (Team page → Open folder) with ordinary documents you can edit.

Tell them about you

Fill in their Profile files, or just say it once: "Sara, I'm allergic to penicillin", "Victor, I earn 60,000 a month after tax". They read these facts at the start of every conversation and keep them up to date.

  • Sara: your health profile and lab values.
  • Victor: what you own and owe, your monthly budget, your investment rules.
  • Ryan: your projects and deadlines.
  • Maya: your routine and how you write, so her drafts sound like you.

Teach them your own skills

Put a short document in a member's Skills folder: the first line says what it is for, then the steps you want followed. They use it whenever a task matches.

  • Ryan: "Weekly status report for my team".
  • Sara: "My diabetes check-in".
  • Victor: "Monthly budget review" with your own categories and limits.
  • Maya: "How I want my inbox sorted".

Give them your material

Your coding standards for Ryan, the ranges printed on your own lab's report for Sara, your country's tax rates for Victor. They use these instead of guessing.

  • Show them documents: lab reports, bills, bank statements, project briefs. They keep them for later.
  • Connect your apps and choose who may use each one.

Correct them, and they learn

"No, I stopped that medicine in June": they update their notes so the mistake doesn't come back.

  • See and edit what each member remembers on the Team page.
  • Choose how they explain things: simple, student or professional.
