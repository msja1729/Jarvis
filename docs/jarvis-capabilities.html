1Jarvis in one page

Jarvis is a desktop app that gives you a small team of AI specialists who know your life in their field. You talk to them by typing or by voice, from any app, and they work in the apps you connect, but only in the ways you allow.

Who's on the team

  • Maya: your schedule, email, deadlines, daily plan, reminders, music, and every personal task.
  • Ryan: projects and coding (he runs Claude Code on Opus 5.5), meeting minutes, plans and deadlines.
  • Sara: health, food, sleep, exercise, symptoms and medical reports.
  • Victor: budgets, debt, investing basics, purchases, tax and business numbers.
  • Jarvis: the front desk; welcomes you, introduces the team and passes everything to the right person.

What you can do with them

  • Ask questions and get answers based on your numbers and history, not generic advice.
  • Turn emails into deadlines, tasks and reminders; plan your day around your energy.
  • Record a meeting and get minutes plus your action items on a board.
  • Have code written, tested and reviewed on your projects while you do something else.
  • Learn anything with narrated, animated lessons, diagrams and interactive maths.

What makes Jarvis different from a normal AI chat

JarvisWhat it means for you
Specialists, not one generalistEach member follows an expert's method and step-by-step playbooks for their field, asks the questions that matter, and pushes back when facts are missing.
They remember youEach member keeps structured files about you (Sara: health profile and lab history; Victor: balance sheet, cash flow, investment rules; Ryan: roadmap; Maya: your schedule and writing style) and uses them every time.
They act, not just talkRead and draft email, add calendar events, set reminders, manage a project board, record meetings, play music, run coding work, read your documents.
Always within reachPush-to-talk from any app, a small voice card above the taskbar, a floating chat box, natural human-sounding voices, and the music pauses while you talk.
You stay in controlEvery action goes through your permission settings (Off / Ask me first / Allowed); anything that sends, deletes or spends always asks; everything is logged.
Private by designYour chats, memory, files, voice and sign-ins stay on your computer. It runs on your own Claude Pro or Max plan; no extra AI subscription.

2Getting started

What you need

ComputerWindows 10 or 11 (64-bit). A Mac version is planned.
Claude planA Claude Pro or Max plan. Jarvis uses Claude Code with your own sign-in; it helps you install and sign in during setup.
Graphics card (recommended)An NVIDIA graphics card makes speech recognition nearly instant (about 0.2 seconds per sentence). Without one, voice still works but is slower.
Disk spaceAbout 500 MB for the app, plus the voice and speech models you choose to download (natural voices about 326 MB; speech recognition about 1.3 GB on NVIDIA computers), plus Claude Code if you don't have it.
MicrophoneOptional; needed to talk to the team and to record meetings.

Installing

  1. Download Jarvis-Setup-0.1.0.exe from the GitHub releases page and run it.
  2. Windows may show "Windows protected your PC" because the installer isn't code-signed yet: click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Choose where to install (or keep the default) and click Install. Jarvis opens when it finishes.

The welcome

The first time Jarvis opens, it walks you through setup with a short animated welcome:

StepWhat happens
Title cardThe Jarvis ring forms from particles, the core lights up and the name appears. Click Let's set you up.
1. Your nameWhat the team should call you. Every member learns it.
2. Connect your ClaudeTwo live checks: Claude Code installed and Signed in. Buttons open a window that installs Claude Code or signs you in through your browser. The page ticks each one by itself. Jarvis never sees or stores your Claude login.
3. Your team's toolsSwitches for web search, lessons, documents, meeting recorder, screen look, reminders, project board and the coding supervisor, plus a microphone test with a live level bar.
4. VoicesDownloads the natural voices and speech recognition with progress bars (you can skip and do it later), and asks how to say your name: Claude suggests a spelling the voice reads correctly, and you can play it and adjust it.

The introductions are written fresh by Claude for you, warm and a little playful. Mute and Skip are always available.

You can replay the introductions (Settings → Meet the team again) or run the whole setup again at any time.

3Talking to Jarvis

Four ways to talk

WhereHowGood for
Main windowOpen Jarvis → ChatLonger conversations, history, lessons, everything at once.
Push-to-talkCtrl+Alt+Space from any appTalk without switching windows: press, speak, and it stops listening when you pause (or press again).
Voice cardAppears above the taskbar; Ctrl+Alt+J shows or hides itHands-free answers: the reply is spoken; the card stays hidden unless you want to read it. Hiding it doesn't stop the speech.
Floating boxCtrl+Shift+SpaceA small chat window that floats over your work.

Choosing who you talk to

Start your message with a member's name and you stay with them until you name someone else:

  • Sara, I've had a headache since yesterday. → Sara answers, and your next messages (it gets worse in the evening) stay with her.
  • Victor, can I afford a new laptop? → now you're with Victor.
  • Job titles work too: doctor, …, PA, …, project manager, …, accountant, ….
  • Names misheard by speech recognition still work (Sarah → Sara, Viktor → Victor), and names you change on the Team page work the same way.
  • You can also click a member's chip above the chat box. Your very first message, before you've named anyone, goes to Maya.

Natural voices

  • 21 natural, human-sounding voices (American and British, male and female) that run on your computer.
  • Each member has their own voice, which you can change on the Team page (with a Play sample button).
  • Replies start speaking while they are still being written, so you hear the first sentence in a few seconds.
  • Spoken questions get short, spoken-style answers; ask for detail and you get more.
  • You can adjust the speaking speed, or switch speech off (Settings, or say mute / unmute).

Speech recognition that understands everyone

  • Uses Whisper large-v3-turbo, a strong model that copes with fast, casual speech and many accents (Indian, British, American, European, East Asian and more).
  • On computers with an NVIDIA graphics card, a sentence turns into text in about 0.2 seconds. In tests with fast, rough speech in ten accents, it made about 40% fewer mistakes than the previous model.
  • The silence around your words is trimmed and soft or rushed speech is evened out before recognition.
  • The team's names and yours are given as hints, so they're spelled correctly.
  • Everything happens on your computer: your voice is never uploaded.

Your name, said right

Voices read names by English spelling rules, so many names (Indian names especially) come out wrong. Jarvis keeps a "say it like" spelling for your name, for example Priyanka → Pree-yaan-kaa. Claude suggests one, you press Hear it and adjust it until it sounds right (during setup, or in Settings → You). Only the sound changes; your name is still written correctly.

Music pauses while you talk

When you start talking, anything playing (Spotify, YouTube in your browser, VLC and most media apps) pauses. It stays paused while the team answers, then resumes on its own. It also pauses when the team speaks on their own (reminders, the morning plan, lessons, coding updates). Only what Jarvis paused is resumed. You can switch this off in Settings.

Instant commands (no AI needed)

5Maya: personal assistant

● Maya Personal assistant and chief of staff · protects your time and energy

Maya looks after your day and everything personal no other member covers: errands, bills, travel, bookings, important dates and forms. She plans around your energy, turns email into action, and makes sure nothing sneaks up on you.

Her skills

Inbox triageSorts email into four tiers: Act now (she drafts the reply right away), Needs a teammate (she asks Ryan, Victor or Sara for the facts, then drafts), Read later (a short daily digest) and Noise (just counted). Every deadline becomes a board task with reminders; likely phishing is flagged; nothing is sent without your OK.
Daily planBuilds your day around your energy: the hardest task in your deep-work window, admin in your low-energy slot, buffers between meetings, meals and breaks, your top 3 priorities. She pushes back when low-value tasks land in your best hours, and adds reminders 10 minutes before meetings.
Weekly reviewWhat got done, what slipped, the next two weeks (meetings, exams, deadlines, birthdays, bills, travel) and next week's priorities.
Email writingDrafts that sound like you, matching your greeting, sign-off, tone and sentence length from your own example emails, and never using AI-sounding phrases ("I hope this email finds you well").
Personal adminRunning lists (to-do, shopping, renewals, important dates), reminders well ahead of bills and renewals, travel research and packing lists, and the documents you need for forms. She never books, pays or enters passwords for you.
Meeting prepA short brief before meetings, calls and interviews: purpose, people, recent emails, questions to raise, what to bring.

What she keeps about you

About me (working hours, deep-work window, best time for meetings, protected time, recurring commitments, important people, preferences) and Writing style (your example emails, greeting and sign-off, phrases to avoid). Plus her folders: Plans, Inbox notes, Personal lists.

Her apps

Gmail / OutlookGoogle / Outlook CalendarRemindersProject boardMeeting recorderDocumentsWeb searchTeam folders

Proactive

Turn on her morning plan (Team page → set a time) and every day she goes through your new email, puts deadlines on the board, plans the day, saves it, and tells you the shape of your day out loud. She also says your reminders out loud at the right time, even if the main window is closed.

6Ryan: project manager & tech lead

● Ryan Project manager and tech lead · ruthless about scope, quality and finishing · thinks with Opus 5.5

Ryan runs your projects. He turns ideas into specs and plans, runs the coding with Claude Code in the background, reviews the result, takes meeting minutes, and keeps the roadmap and board current. He uses Opus 5.5, the strongest model for building software.

His skills

Spec gate (coding brief)Before any coding, Ryan writes a SPEC.md for the change (goal, scope, context, design, edge cases, breaking changes and risks, test plan, definition of done, open questions), shows you a summary and waits for your OK. Only then does he start Claude Code, with a brief that includes your coding standards and the tests that must pass.
Scope guardWhen a new idea or change of plans arrives, he scores it (RICE or MoSCoW), checks it against your roadmap and the board's workload, shows what it would push back and by how long, and recommends: do now, schedule next, or park it. He's allowed to say "not now".
Project briefA one-page brief for a new project or feature: problem and users, goals and non-goals, must/should/could, user stories with acceptance criteria, approach, milestones, risks.
Plan breakdownSplits work into tasks of half a day to two days, orders them by dependencies and risk, estimates with three-point estimates plus a buffer, and puts milestones and tasks on the board.
Meeting minutesFrom a recorded meeting: summary, key points, decisions, an action-item table with owners and dates (your own tasks marked), open questions. Your tasks go on the board with reminders, and the project status and roadmap are updated.
Status reportRed, amber or green with the reason, then done, in progress, next, blocked, risks and the decisions he needs from you.
Code reviewCorrectness, security, tests, design, performance and readability, with findings ranked by severity, each with file and line and a concrete fix.

What he keeps

Roadmap (now / next / later, decisions made), plus reference files: Coding standards (your preferred stack and rules), Spec template and Definition of done. Folders: Projects (one folder per project, with Brief, Plan, SPEC, Status, Risks) and Meetings.

His apps

Coding supervisor (Claude Code)Documents & codeProject boardRemindersCalendarsMeeting recorderScreen lookWeb search (official docs)Team foldersApps you add (e.g. GitHub)

7Sara: health & nutrition

● Sara Health coach with a careful clinician's method · not a replacement for an in-person doctor

Sara gives practical, evidence-based help with food, sleep, exercise, stress, everyday symptoms and medical reports, and she reads every result against your own history, not just population ranges.

Her skills

Symptom checkRed flags first (if one fits, she tells you to get emergency help now, before anything else). Then OPQRST questions one or two at a time (onset, what makes it better or worse, what it feels like, where, how bad, how it changes), then 2–4 likely explanations, a clear triage level (emergency / today / within days / self-care), safe self-care, and "get help sooner if…".
Lab reportsReads the whole report, records every value in your biomarker table (including values you just tell her), compares with your previous results to spot trends, and reads patterns, not single numbers. She points out urgent values and gives you questions for your doctor. In testing, given HbA1c values of 5.6 → 5.9 → 6.1 she said "the steady upward trend matters more than any single number" and linked it to the family history and habits in the health profile.
Nutrition planCalorie needs from your body and activity, protein targets, the plate method, meals from your own cuisine in household measures, a shopping list, and swaps instead of bans; adapted for diabetes, blood pressure, pregnancy or allergies.
Exercise planA safety screen first, then a realistic weekly plan (frequency, intensity, time, type) that builds up gradually, including strength work.
Sleep and stressThe proven methods behind CBT for insomnia, sleep habits, a 2-week sleep diary, and short stress techniques; plus when to see a professional.
Medicine informationWhat a medicine does, how to take it, common versus serious side effects and interactions, from trusted sources. She never changes a prescribed dose.

What she keeps about you

Health profile (age, conditions, medicines, allergies, baseline blood pressure and weight, lifestyle, goals, what your doctor said) and the Biomarker table (every lab value with date, unit and the lab's range). Reference files: Red flags, Lab ranges (your report's own range always wins) and a Preventive screening schedule; she tells you which screenings apply to you now.

Her apps

Web search (medical authorities only: WHO, NIH, NHS, Mayo Clinic…)Documents (reports, PDFs)LessonsScreen lookTeam folders

Safety lines Sara never crosses: she doesn't diagnose with certainty, never changes prescribed medication, and always sends you to emergency services or a doctor for warning signs (chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, fainting, heavy bleeding, thoughts of self-harm).

8Victor: money & business

● Victor Money and business analyst who works from your real numbers · information, not regulated advice

Victor turns money questions into clear numbers: what you can afford, what a decision really costs, and whether it fits your goals. He shows the maths and the assumptions, and holds you to the investment rules you set while you were calm.

His skills

Purchase evaluator"Should I buy this?": the price as a share of your take-home pay, months of savings it uses, the effect on your emergency fund, opportunity cost (what the money would grow to if invested), the interest if on EMI, cost per use, cheaper alternatives, and whether it delays a goal. Then a clear verdict (buy now / wait / skip) with the deciding numbers.
Investment policyWrites your own rulebook: goals, horizon, risk you can live with, target allocation, rebalancing rule, and rules for bad days (what you promise not to do in a market fall or a hype rally). Later, when you want to act on fear or hype, he holds you to it.
BudgetPicks a method that fits you (50/30/20, zero-based, pay-yourself-first), checks your emergency fund, builds a planned-vs-actual table, finds the 2–3 biggest savings with amounts, and can categorise a bank statement you give him.
Debt payoffEMI and total interest, avalanche vs snowball compared with your numbers, prepaying vs investing, refinancing or consolidating, and warning signs.
Investing basicsThe right order (emergency fund, high-interest debt, insurance, then goals), allocation by goal and horizon, low-cost diversified funds, compounding shown at realistic returns after inflation, and the usual traps.
Tax basicsYour country's current rules looked up on official sources, saved with the date and link, a worked estimate with your numbers, and deadlines as reminders through Maya.
Business analysisUnit economics (margin, break-even, customer acquisition cost vs lifetime value), market size from the bottom up, pricing, a 12-month projection with worst/base/best cases, and the 3 assumptions to test first.

What he keeps about you

Balance sheet (assets, liabilities with rates and EMIs, net worth), Monthly cash flow (income, fixed and variable costs, savings rate) and Investment policy. Reference files: Formulas (EMI, compounding, monthly investing, real returns, opportunity cost, emergency-fund cover, debt-to-income, break-even) and Rates and rules, which only ever holds figures he has verified on official sources, with the date.

His apps

Web search (official sources for rates and tax)Documents (statements, spreadsheets, PDFs)Lessons and chartsScreen lookTeam folders

Safety lines: general information, not regulated financial advice (for big decisions he suggests a licensed, fee-only adviser). He never asks for passwords, card numbers, one-time codes or bank logins.

9What makes the team experts

Each member is built on Claude: the same knowledge and reasoning. What turns them into specialists is everything around the model:

IngredientWhat it gives you
An expert methodEvery member works the same way: start from your files → if a fact that changes the answer is missing, ask 1–3 specific questions first (never generic advice) → follow the right skill → update your profile → end with one concrete next step. No filler.
Skills (playbooks)27 step-by-step procedures across the team (listed in sections 5–8), so the expert process is followed every time without you writing a careful prompt.
Profile filesStructured facts about you in each field, loaded automatically at the start of every conversation and kept up to date as you talk.
Reference filesChecked material they must use instead of guessing: red flags, lab ranges, screening schedules, finance formulas, verified tax rates, spec templates, definition of done.
MemoryNotes about you, summarised as you talk. You can read, edit, summarise or erase them on the Team page.
Their own folderA real folder on your computer (Team → PA, Project Manager, Doctor, Finance) where they keep plans, minutes, notes, budgets and research, organised by date. Open it from the Team page.
Trusted sourcesWeb search steered to the right sites for each field (medical authorities, official regulators, official software docs).
The right tools and modelOnly the apps their job needs, and a stronger model where it pays off (Ryan on Opus 5.5).

Make them yours

  • Add your own skills: drop a Markdown file in a member's Skills folder (a title line, then the steps or rules) and they'll use it.
  • Fill in or correct their files directly: they're ordinary documents in their folders, and your edits are never overwritten.
  • Tell them things once: Victor, I earn 60,000 a month after tax and pay 15,000 rent, and it goes into his cash flow for every future answer.

10Lessons and visual explanations

When something is clearer when seen, a member explains it with a lesson: a narrated, step-by-step presentation that opens in its own window on the empty side of your screen (or full screen), with each picture appearing exactly as the voice explains it.

What a lesson can show

Diagrams and charts

  • Flowcharts, process and relationship diagrams
  • Bar, line and pie charts
  • Tables, notes and highlighted key points
  • Moving scenes that animate a process

Visual maths

  • Curves that draw themselves; a tangent that slides as a value changes
  • Shaded areas and rectangles that fill the area under a curve
  • Vectors and a whole grid that bends under a matrix
  • Equations that transform step by step, each quantity in its own colour
  • Interactive: drag points, move sliders, scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and replay any step.
  • References: quotes from a document, pieces of code, or a cut-out of your screen, numbered with a reference list at the end.
  • Adapts to the question: a simple question gets a plain answer; an idea gets a short lesson; a problem or a deep topic gets four parts: Problem → Intuition → Solution → Recap.
  • At your level: Settings → "How Jarvis explains things": Automatic, Like I'm five, Student, or Professional (precise terms, trade-offs, real code). You can also just say explain like I'm five.
  • Controls: Space pause, arrow keys step, F side or full screen, Esc close; push-to-talk pauses the lesson so you can ask a follow-up.
  • The lesson is narrated in the voice of the member who made it. Sara uses lessons to show how the body works, Victor to chart budgets and compounding.

11Documents and code

  • Open a file or folder you pick (a window asks you which; a member never browses your computer on their own): PDFs, text, spreadsheets as text, and whole code projects.
  • Read only what's needed: pages of a PDF or ranges of lines, with answers that cite where they came from (page numbers, file and line).
  • Search an opened folder for words or code.
  • Secret files are never opened (keys, passwords, .env files).
  • Typical uses: Sara, explain this blood report · Victor, categorise this bank statement · Ryan, how does login work in this project?

12Looking at your screen

  • Ask what's this error on my screen? or explain this chart and a member takes one screenshot for that request.
  • You choose what to share (a window, a whole screen, or an area you draw), and a small indicator shows when your screen is being looked at.
  • The member only ever looks when you ask; never in the background.

13Meetings and recordings

  • Ryan, record this meeting and note down the tasks given to me: records your microphone and your computer's sound (the other people on the call, a video you play), and transcribes it on your computer as the meeting goes on.
  • The screen too (on by default, Settings → Meeting recorder): a full-HD video of the screen with all the sound, plus a sharp picture each time the screen changes and then holds still: a new slide, a shared document, writing on a whiteboard. A playing video is not photographed frame by frame. The transcript marks when each picture was shown.
  • When writing the notes, the member looks at those pictures (slide titles, figures, diagrams, what was written on the board) and puts what was shown into the minutes.
  • When you say stop recording, the member you asked writes it up in their own style:
    • Ryan (also the default): minutes, decisions, an action-item table, your tasks on the board with due dates and reminders, project status and roadmap updated.
    • Sara (Sara, record this appointment): what the doctor found, tests, medicines with doses, instructions, warning signs and the follow-up date; reminders through Maya.
    • Maya or Victor: notes with every task, deadline, figure and decision that involves you.
  • The notes are saved in that member's folder and the transcript stays on your computer. The video and pictures are saved in your Videos → Jarvis Meetings folder (about 14 MB a minute) and are kept even if you uninstall Jarvis; with the screen recording off, no audio is kept after transcription.
  • Always tell people when you record them; the rules differ between countries.

15Reminders, morning plan and project board

16Coding supervisor

Ryan runs real coding work on your projects with Claude Code, in the background, and acts like a good team lead between you and it. (Turn it on in the Tool Hub; it's off by default.)

  1. Ryan, add a login page to my project: you pick the project folder.
  2. Ryan writes the spec and waits for your OK (section 6).
  3. Claude Code works in the background while you do something else. Safe steps (reading, editing project files, running tests and builds) are approved for you according to your setting.
  4. When Claude has a question or wants to do something risky, a small card appears (and is spoken) with Ryan's recommendation and reason. Answer by clicking or by voice: go with yours, the second one, no, keep it.
  5. You get progress updates while it works, and a summary at the end with the test results and the list of changed files; Ryan checks it against the spec and updates the board.
SettingOptions
How much Ryan may approveCareful (asks for any change), Balanced (approves low-risk steps), Hands-off (approves low and medium risk). High-risk actions (deleting, pushing to git, installing packages, network access, anything outside the project, key or password files) always ask you.
UpdatesEvery few minutes or only at milestones; spoken or silent.
Your own sessionsOptionally, Jarvis tells you when Claude Code sessions you started yourself (VS Code, terminal) finish or need you.

You can also give more instructions while it runs (also add a "remember me" box), ask for progress, stop it, or open the project in VS Code.

17Web search

  • Built in for every member; no account needed. Search the web and read pages for current facts, prices, news and documentation.
  • Steered to trusted sources by field: Sara uses medical authorities (WHO, NIH, MedlinePlus, NHS, Mayo Clinic, CDC); Victor uses official government and regulator sites; Ryan uses official documentation; others use reliable general sources.
  • Members name their sources and only give links that actually came back from the search, never made-up links.
  • Web pages are treated as information only; instructions on a page are never followed.

18Apps and the Tool Hub

The Tool Hub is where you choose which apps your team can use, and exactly what they may do in each.

For every app

  • On / off switch.
  • For every ability: Off / Ask me first / Allowed. Anything destructive (like deleting) can never be set to Allowed.
  • Who can use it: coloured chips for Maya, Ryan, Sara and Victor; click to give or take away an app (for example, give your calendar to Sara for appointment planning). Jarvis always has every app. Reset puts back the usual members.

The apps

  • Email and calendar: Gmail and Google Calendar (Google account), Outlook mail and Outlook Calendar (Microsoft account).
  • Built in, nothing to set up: web search, lessons, documents & code, meeting recorder, screen look, reminders, project board, team folders, skills, team (members asking each other) and the coding supervisor.
  • Turn each one on or off; nothing else can be added in this version.

19Conversations and history

  • Chats are organised into conversations. A new one starts when you open Jarvis, after 30 quiet minutes, or when you click New chat.
  • The History panel next to the chat lists them by Today, Yesterday, Previous 7 days, Previous 30 days and by month, each with a title, time and the coloured dots of who took part.
  • Search finds words in any old conversation.
  • Open an earlier conversation to read it (with dates between days), and Continue it: each member picks up exactly where they left off.
  • Select and delete conversations, or a whole day, for good. Chats from the voice card and floating box go into the same conversation.
  • Settings: keep history on this computer (on by default), or switch it off and clear everything.

20Activity

  • Everything your team did in your apps, grouped by day: time, who did it (with their colour), what they did and in which app, and the result (Done, Blocked, You said no, Failed).

21Safety, permissions and privacy

You stay in control

  • Every action passes your permission settings before it happens: Off, Ask me first, or Allowed, per ability, per app.
  • Anything that sends, deletes, spends or acts asks you first with a clear description of exactly what will happen. Deleting can never be set to Allowed.
  • Members only use the apps you give them (Who can use it). Asking for anything else is blocked.
  • Everything is logged in Activity.
  • Content is never obeyed: text inside emails, documents, web pages, transcripts and screenshots is treated as information, never as instructions, even if it claims to come from you.
  • Coding work never skips permission checks; risky steps always come to you.

Your data stays yours

On your computerChats and history, members' memory and folders, profiles, reminders, the board, transcripts, meeting videos and screen pictures (in your Videos folder), voices and speech recognition, settings.
In the Windows Credential ManagerYour sign-ins to Google, Microsoft and added apps, and app keys. Never in files.
Sent to AnthropicYour requests to the team go to Claude on your own plan, through Claude Code. Jarvis never reads or stores your Claude login.
Sent to other servicesOnly what you use: web searches go to the search service (Exa); email and calendar actions go to Google or Microsoft; added apps go to their own service.
Never uploadedYour voice. Speech recognition and the natural voices run on your computer.

Health and money safety

Sara is not a doctor and Victor is not a licensed adviser. They give careful, evidence-based information, say clearly when something needs a professional, and send you to emergency help for warning signs.

23Installing, updating and uninstalling

  • Install: run the installer; choose the folder; Jarvis opens and the welcome guides you (section 2).
  • Update: run a newer installer over the old one. Your chats, memory, folders, settings and sign-ins are kept.
  • Uninstall: Settings → Uninstall Jarvis, or Windows Settings → Apps → Jarvis. Uninstalling removes the app and everything Jarvis kept: settings, chats, memory, team folders, voices and speech models, saved sign-ins in the Windows Credential Manager, the Claude Code hooks it added, and its own saved Claude sessions, as if it had never been installed. Claude Code itself, your own projects and your meeting videos (Videos → Jarvis Meetings) are not touched.

Disk space after a full setup: about 1 GB without speech recognition models; about 2.6 GB on an NVIDIA computer with natural voices and fast speech recognition; plus Claude Code (about 350 MB) if it wasn't already installed.
